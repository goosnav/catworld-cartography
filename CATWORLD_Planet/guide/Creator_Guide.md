# CATWORLD / TERRA - CREATOR & SHOWRUNNER GUIDE

Revision 10. Prepared 1 November 2026.

## What is established, and what is proposed

### Established geography

Terra has a 4,000-Earth-year orbit. The creator requested stronger axial tilt; this model uses a proposed 35 degrees. Panterra and the eastern continent share the giant mountain system. The separate southern mainland is closer to Panterra and has a sparse eastern island chain. No island arc links it to the eastern continent.

### Current epoch

The north is in late autumn and its high country is already wintry. The south is in late spring approaching summer. The older southern-winter crossing and current southward bird movement remain the story framework.

### Authoritative water order

The northern mountain river crosses the upper plateau, falls into the reservoir valley, and supplies the lake. A separate, slightly more southerly river drains west through Seattle country to the sea. The eastern megadam and its downstream city remain in place. This replaces the earlier Seattle-into-lake layout.

### Regional environments

Most of northern Panterra is a broad mountain province. Steppe occupies the lower northern foothills. The west is extensive forest country around Seattle. Southern settled districts are fertile farmland and rolling hills.

### Design proposals and limits

Radius 6,100 km, 35-degree tilt, 24-hour rotation, Ls 255 degrees, a 65 m former lowstand and an illustrative crossing 1,100 years ago remain proposals. Exact ice extent, heat source, water budget and engineering need further development. This is a consistent drawing-reference model, not a climate or engineering simulation.

## The planet at a glance

### Three continental identities

Panterra is the main story landscape. The eastern dry core remains attached through the mountain belt, rotated as one body, with a continuous mountain connection. A deep ocean separates it from the independent southern shield.

### The southern silhouette

The smaller mainland sits southwest of Panterra, closer and farther south after a further 10-degree eastward and 1-degree southward adjustment in R7. An asymmetric headland, oblique old escarpment, deeply cut southeastern coast and offset southern peninsulas replace the inverted-Australia resemblance.

### An intentionally sparse route

Five retained islands lead east toward Panterra: two large fragments, one middle island and two small remnants. The largest, irregular continental fragments cluster near the southern mainland; small, separated arc remnants pepper the approach to Panterra. Large rifted fragments recall Oceania; elongated island forms recall the Aleutians. Those are visual analogues, not copies of their real climates.

### Using the globe

Front is 0 degrees, east +90, west -90 and far side 180. The southern regional camera follows the relocated mainland. Polar and regional views all render the same model.

## What changed in this pass

### Whole-continent placement

The entire eastern plate is rotated rigidly by 23 degrees on the sphere from the intact R8 baseline. One rotation moves its coastline, rift gulf, peninsulas and terrain together; it preserves spherical distances and proportions. It replaces the rejected R9 elastic bend, rather than adding another deformation to it.

### One continuous mountain system

The junction is reconstructed as coastal foothills, dissected ranges and high valleys. The suture follows a continuous southeast course into the eastern interior without the former V-shaped reversal. Bounded mountain elevation samples provide branching relief through the join. Existing relief away from the junction is retained; broad blur filters no longer turn high terrain into blank snowfields. Snow, rock, vegetation and slope shading are recalculated at the new latitude.

### The rest of the atlas

The two 8K rectangular maps, curved five-island crossing, Pelagic Crown and scattered ocean islands are retained. Panterra's reservoir, Seattle outlet, northern feeder and agricultural districts keep their established layout. Tectonic sheets, regional cameras and landmark coordinates follow the revised continent.

## A collision system and a separate old continent

### Older continental crust

Panterra, the eastern dry interior and the southern mainland preserve older crustal blocks. The northern impact and its iron province predate the main collision. The southern shield has its own weathering, rifting and erosion history.

### The main collision stops at the eastern margin

Panterra and the eastern block collide, thickening crust and supporting the gargantuan range. Broad folds, plateaus and valleys explain the mountain bridge. This belt does not need to continue across every continent. [S1]

### Southern rifting and an old raised margin

The independent southern continent is interpreted as an older rifted block. Its eastern escarpment is a rejuvenated inherited mountain margin, lower than the main collision belt. Extension breaks the continental shelf into large islands, gulfs and straits. This supports an Australia-like role without copying Australia.

### Two different seaways

Deep ocean separates the southern continent from the eastern dry core. The long eastern shelf ribbon toward Panterra is a distinct, shallow remnant of stretched continental crust. Its former emergence is a sea-level hypothesis, not a new mountain collision. Boundary lines are schematic and plate velocities are unspecified.

## Gargantuan ranges, not a row of peaks

### A northern province, not one ridge

Most northern Panterran land outside the impact basin is now strongly mountainous. Multiple folded ranges and rugged massifs surround high valleys and inherited upland basins. The main crest remains the highest tier; broad supporting terrain makes it part of a continent.

### A plausible mountain history

Long convergence thickens crust across a wide belt, while erosion and faulting separate individual ridges and basins. Renewed uplift can affect older northern crust without making the ancient impact responsible for every mountain. [S1]

### Relief and erosion texture

A Himalayan elevation analogue supplies coherent ridge-and-valley structure at several scales. Broad foothill transitions and coastal taper prevent mountains from becoming a uniformly extruded wall. The eastern range is rotated with its host terrain.

### How to draw it

Globe relief is exaggerated ten times. Draw a wide region of overlapping ranges and deep valleys, then step down into open foothills. Use the local dam and waterfall studies for true-height relationships.

### Southern mountain groups

The creator's two southern mountain regions are built as broad older uplifts and a rugged peninsula spine. Eroded fold roots and later fault uplift explain high ground separated from the active northern collision. Their lower slopes feed tributaries and grade into the settled agricultural country.

## Panterra: the principal story landscape

### Four readable regions

A mountain-dominated north descends into steppe foothills and a dissected plateau. The great lake occupies a valley inside that plateau. Westward lie Seattle forest country and its river corridor. Southward lie fertile settlements, pasture and rolling agricultural hills.

### A substantial western forest

Old growth covers a broad western watershed and inland hills, with wet ravines, logging tributaries, clearings and settlement margins. The forest gradually opens toward the plateau; it is not a thin painted coastline.

### Farming and settlement

The southern green-and-gold lowlands represent agricultural country around rolling hills and the two southern mountain groups. Alluvial reaches and sheltered foothills explain settlement. High rocky interiors remain uncultivated. Individual fields and buildings are below globe resolution.

### Defence and movement

The broad mountains retain lower passes and staging valleys. Rivers, escarpments and coastlines channel movement without implying that every route is easily navigable.

## Two river corridors and an eastern dam

### Northern feeder

Mountain runoff gathers northwest of the lake, northeast of Seattle. The main feeder follows the upper plateau and reaches the lake through a major waterfall and a single drowned valley arm. It follows the marked western plateau route instead of the crossed-out eastern route.

### Western Seattle outlet

A high natural bedrock sill allows overflow from the reservoir into the western river. The river then loses elevation gradually through forest country toward Seattle and its estuary. It lies south of the northern feeder.

### The eastern outlet remains

The megadam blocks the deeper eastern route. Controlled releases descend to the eastern downstream metropolis and sea. The reservoir therefore has a natural western overflow and a managed eastern outlet, not two incoming main rivers.

### Readable watercourses

The main feeder, western outlet and eastern release dominate the globe. A few converging tributaries follow the marked western and southern catchments, with three small outlying lakes and a restrained estuary fork. Blue lines are widened for drawing clarity, not literal channel widths.

## A continental lake needs a continental basin

### A valley inside a plateau

The water surface is approximately 690 m above sea level. The surrounding plateau is higher, with foothills and mountains beyond. Before the dam, a smaller lake and river occupied the low valley floor; impoundment flooded connected valley branches.

### Why one dam can create this lake

The eastern channel was the lower escape route. Blocking that gorge raises water until it reaches the higher western saddle. The west can then overflow naturally without draining the reservoir back to its pre-dam floor. The selected western sill is about 685 m; normal water is above it.

### Why the eastern dam is lower

The downstream eastern valley floor is much lower than the surrounding plateau. The dam foundation and tailwater sit closer to sea level, while the dam crest still rises above the 690 m reservoir. A low foundation does not mean a low crest.

### Water budget and filling time

The continental reservoir needs continental runoff and a long filling history. The northern river is the prominent feeder; smaller catchments are suppressed visually. Evaporation, western overflow and eastern releases must balance over time. The previous one-outlet discharge estimate cannot be assigned independently to both outlets.

### Freshness and navigation

Throughflow can export dissolved salts, while sediment accumulates in lake arms. A broad lake or river is not automatically navigable end to end; falls, rapids, portage and seasonal operating levels need local story decisions.

## The Great Panterran Dam

### The designed object

The separate inset has a 4.8 km crest, approximately 470 m structural height, reservoir level at 690 m and crest near 715 m. It includes intake towers, bronze gates, spillways, turbine halls, tailraces, access roads, checkpoints and a fishing village. One inset unit equals one kilometre.

### Power comes from head and flow

Using P = density x gravity x flow x head x efficiency, an assumed 10,000 m3/s, 350 m net head and 90% efficiency gives about 31 GW. A nominal 60 GW would require roughly 19,400 m3/s at that head. Lake size alone cannot guarantee that discharge. [S7]

### Scale and limits

The concept is larger in structural height than Hoover (about 221 m) and Three Gorges (181 m). Its illustrative 31 GW exceeds Three Gorges' 22.5 GW installed capacity. These comparisons establish ambition, not engineering feasibility. The inset is a fictional design study, not a construction-ready dam. [S8, S9]

### Global and local scales

The dam wall is below the globe texture resolution. The local study shows the abrupt drop; the global river grade is smoothed. The village terrace is resolved in the inset, not in its approximate global anchor. Do not measure the local gorge from the planetary mesh.

## The northern plateau waterfall

### A 400 m change of level

The mountain-fed river runs across the high plateau at about 1,090 m near its end. A 250 m main waterfall reaches an 840 m plunge pool; lower cascades carry the flow down to the 690 m reservoir.

### Why the waterfall is here

The river crosses a resistant bedrock lip where the upper plateau is cut by the deeper valley. Hard layers hold ledges while softer or fractured rock erodes below them. The drowned valley arm reaches the foot of the cascade.

### How to use the study

The local Blender model shows a continuous water surface meeting its banks, the main curtain, pools, lower cascades, steppe above and limited riparian vegetation. It is a landform reference, not a fluid simulation or a precise cutout of the coarse globe mesh.

### Story implications

This is a barrier to through-navigation and a natural crossing, portage and lookout landmark. Noise, spray and seasonal discharge affect its surroundings. Its long-term erosion rate remains unspecified.

## The northern impact and the red sea

### An old scar incorporated into a continent

The basin has a central uplift, a broken rim and a broad apron that merges into the northern mainland. It is older than the collision belt. Erosion and subsequent tectonics modify its edges, so it reads as part of the crust rather than a circular object placed on top.

### Why the red works

Rust-coloured soil and sediment suggest oxidized iron minerals such as hematite. Red water represents suspended mineral-rich material and staining, not liquid metal. Colour is a strong visual cue, but it does not prove that the iron came from a meteorite. [S3]

### Ore and the story

The iron-rich northern province is a creator premise. Impact melting, fractured rock and later fluid circulation offer a proposed way to redistribute and concentrate resources. Real impact structures can host ore deposits, but rare earths and enriched uranium are not automatic products of an iron-rich impact. Keep those deck claims as separate fiction decisions. [S4; D5, D47]

### Time to recover

A country-scale impact implies an ancient catastrophe. Its age must allow climate and ecosystems to recover before the current bird kingdoms. The exact age, original diameter and ore inventory remain unspecified.

## The dry eastern core

### A coherent southward orientation

The eastern continent uses one rigid 23-degree rotation from the intact R8 position, about 30 degrees east / 37 degrees north. The whole coast and interior turn together. The joining mountains are reconstructed separately across the suture to produce a continuous southeast range. This is a cartographic placement operation, not a claim that a plate physically rotated overnight.

### Relief and rain shadow

The enlarged northern mountain margin gives way to dry lee-side country with gravel basins, dunes, salt pans and dissected escarpments. The west/left dry side from the creator redline remains dry. Coastal pockets can still receive rain.

### Coasts with a history

The long rift gulf, irregular peninsulas and exposed faulted margins move with the continent. Rivers and geographic anchors are transformed with the land, avoiding detached overlays.

### Open ocean beyond

There is no continuous bridge or connecting island arc between this continent and the southern mainland. Isolated oceanic volcanic islands do not close that deep-water separation. Their geology and migration roles are distinct.

## The independent southern continent

### An independent southern world

The redesigned shield remains entirely south of the equator. Its angular northwestern headland, asymmetric southeast embayment and offset southern peninsulas give it a new outline rather than a rotated Earth analogue.

### Dry interior, wet margins

Old weathered uplands, stony basins and salt pans occupy the interior. The eastern escarpment catches moisture; the cooler southern coast has wetter inlets and slopes. These climatic contrasts persist during southern spring.

### Closer, but still separate

The southeastward adjustment shortens the passage toward Panterra and puts the mainland more clearly to its southwest. Water still clearly separates the modern mainland and the island chain. There is no new land neck.

### Old homeland

Its identity as the cats' old homeland is still a proposal. The sheltered eastern shelf, coastal resources and former crossing can support that history without fixing political borders or final place names.

## The eastern archipelago and the old crossing

### A sparse curved archipelago

Five islands replace the crowded belt; the southern mainland is closer, shortening the crossing. Larger fault-bounded fragments transition into slender volcanic-looking islands, separated by conspicuous water gaps. The chain follows a pronounced bend: nearly north-south near the Panterran peninsula, then westward toward the southern mainland. The Aleutians inform its arc curvature and aligned peaks, not the depth of its crossing shelf. [S14]

### What could form a former bridge

Submerged continental saddles provide the possible old crossing. The volcanic-looking peaks alone do not imply shallow water: the model separately includes a continental shelf beneath the route. At the illustrative 65 m lower sea level, that designed shelf emerges.

### Ice and sea level

Net growth of grounded ice can lower the ocean. Floating sea ice alone is insufficient. Strong southern winter could support the crossing only if added southern land ice outweighed losses elsewhere. This remains a hypothesis. [S11]

### Limits

Sparse islands do not prove that the whole gap was once walkable. The lowstand test verifies designed connectivity, not real ice volume, dates, currents, or travel safety. The global grid cannot specify every historical strait.

## Two poles with different identities

### A darker northern winter frontier

With 35-degree tilt, the polar circles lie at 55 degrees latitude. At the chosen late-autumn epoch, the Blood Lake district is already in geometric polar night. Reference cameras remain lit for drawing; they do not represent the actual sun position.

### Northern ice and relief

Snow covers high ranges and spreads across northern uplands. Ocean pack and broken shore ice extend around the polar margins. The iron-red lake remains a readable design landmark; the exact amount of unfrozen water needs a thermal and salinity explanation.

### Southern spring

Grounded polar ice and mountain glaciers remain while coastal seas reopen. More extreme tilt also brings stronger summers, so a permanent ice map cannot be inferred from winter severity alone.

### Ice storage and migration

The large southern polar landmass can store grounded ice. Whether its long winter actually produced the proposed lowstand requires a global mass balance. Opposite seasons by themselves do not establish the crossing.

## Climate follows terrain and exposure

### Western moisture and old growth

The western country is the main forest province. Maritime moisture, wet ravines and sheltered lower slopes support dense stands around Seattle. Forest coverage fades gradually inland rather than ending at a rectangular boundary.

### Northern foothill steppe

Open grassland and scrub occupy the northern foothills below the high mountains and the drier plateau interior. Water follows valleys; narrow riparian vegetation does not turn the whole region into forest.

### Fertile southern country

The south and major settled districts have agricultural mosaics, pasture and rolling hills. Lake margins and river terraces support crops and transport. Soil fertility is a design premise, not a calculated agronomic yield.

### Rain shadows and model limits

Wet windward slopes and dry lee interiors follow mountain exposure and proposed winds. Seasonal rain belts and currents are schematic. Regional colours combine terrain and climate reasoning but are not a circulation-model result. [S2]

## Living inside thousand-year seasons

### An explicit working date

Use solar longitude 255 degrees: about 833 years after the northern autumn equinox and 167 years before northern winter solstice in a circular 4,000-year orbit. The south is the same distance through spring, approaching summer. These precise numbers are proposed for continuity; the seasonal pairing follows the creator's direction. [S12]

### A stronger tilt

The adopted tilt is 35 degrees. Higher tilt increases summer sunlight and reduces winter sunlight at high latitudes, strengthening seasonal contrasts. The thousand-year duration allows long ecological and human responses, but does not mean temperature declines without limit for a thousand years. [S13]

### Weather does not wait for the boundary

Northern high latitudes can already feel wintry during late autumn. Southern uplands and the polar ice sheet can remain cold during late spring. Latitude, elevation, oceans and inherited ice modify local conditions. A thousand-year season is a changing climate interval, not one daily weather pattern repeated unchanged.

### Earlier crossing and current bird movement

A proposed crossing 1,100 years ago falls within the previous southern winter / northern summer. A lower ocean exposes the shelf while northern destinations are warmer. Today, net southern ice loss has drowned that corridor, while advancing northern cold pushes bird communities south. Exact ice history and migration dates remain to be agreed.

### The heat source is still unresolved

A 4,000-year orbit around a one-solar-mass star has semimajor axis about 252 AU. A Sun-like star would not provide Earth-like warmth there. The setting needs a different stellar/orbital or additional-heating premise. The seasonal visuals assume that a habitable thermal solution exists. [S6]

## The present seasonal distribution

### One stated epoch

Solar longitude remains 255 degrees in the proposed near-circular orbit: about 833 Earth years after the northern autumn equinox and 167 before northern winter solstice. The south is in late spring.

### Daylight is recalculated

The model now derives daylight from 35-degree tilt and the stored epoch. The Blood Lake has zero geometric daylight at this snapshot. The southern mainland has long spring days. The delivered CSV gives region-specific values without atmospheric refraction or terrain horizons.

### Surface appearance versus illumination

Snow, vegetation and sea ice show a proposed seasonal state. Broad daylight lights are used in every reference camera so the geography remains visible. The guide separates that artistic lighting from the planet's actual day/night geometry.

## The cat crossing: then and now

### Then: southern winter / northern summer

The lower panel lowers the ocean by 65 m in the stored elevation field. Exposed shelf links the old southern mainland to Panterra. The route can support generations of settlement and movement, rather than requiring one uninterrupted march. The 1,100-year date places it within the previous southern winter.

### Now: southern spring / northern autumn

The same shelf is drowned and the southern mainland is separate. Islands retain traces of the former route. Northern birds now face cooling homelands and move toward milder Panterra. The present archipelago is a shipping landscape, not a modern dry crossing.

### What the check proves

A periodic-grid connectivity test confirms the designed lowstand geometry. It does not establish the required global ice volume, exact water depth in each local channel, bridge longevity or the historical migration itself. Those remain stated worldbuilding assumptions.

## Resources create routes and conflicts

### The material contrast

The deck's Panterran economy relies on bronze/brass, scarce iron, waterways and electrical metallurgy. Northern birds bring access to an iron-rich province. The map supports that contrast through separate resource districts and costly passages between them. It does not require every useful metal to have the same geological origin. [D47]

### The forest is contested infrastructure

Giant trees supply timber and support sacred avian sites. Rivers make those forests economically accessible to logging interests. Deforestation, damaged spawning streams and lost nesting places can therefore be local, visible causes of conflict rather than abstract background. [D4, D5]

### Two different kinds of water control

The eastern dam controls releases, power and part of the lake-level budget. The natural western overflow responds to water level rather than a gate. Seattle depends on that western river, so lake management affects communities on both sides of the plateau.

### Limits of this atlas

Ore tonnages, forestry yields, crop output, electrical demand and transport capacity are not quantified. The map suggests where these systems interact; it is not a balanced economy simulation. Political ownership and final settlement names remain creator choices.

## Cities and settlements need reasons

### Seattle / forest country and lower-river port

Seattle names a substantial western region as well as its port. Old-growth woods, working forest, tributary settlements and fertile clearings surround the downstream river. Place the core settlement on raised ground near the estuary, with wharves on sheltered reaches. This is no longer the upstream timber-city layout.

### London and Rome / lake country

London occupies southwestern lakeshore country and Rome a high eastern bank. Both can combine fisheries, lake shipping and agricultural hinterlands. Shorelines need harbours, terraces and seasonal operating levels before detailed city plans are drawn.

### Amsterdam / downstream outlet

The large downstream city is a trade and processing center with canal links and alluvial land. Its wealth can come from controlling exchange and infrastructure rather than possessing rich local ore. This follows the deck's trade-outlet concept. [D41]

### Milan / merchant island

The southern island is a useful site for maritime customs, resupply and finance. Its precise role in the inland supply chain still needs chosen routes. Do not assume the lake and sea share one uninterrupted navigable level.

### Small places

Fishing villages belong in sheltered coves and on stable terraces. Crossing towns belong at ferries, bridges and junctions. Checkpoints belong at supply bottlenecks. Ancient ruins should predate the modern routes they overlook. The dam inset shows one village proposal; the planetary model does not contain a city-building kit.

### Travel continuity

At 6,100 km radius, one degree of latitude is about 106 km. Several degrees between markers can mean weeks of travel, depending on transport and terrain. Route distances, seasonal pass closures and communication speeds must be set before simultaneous episode journeys are scripted.

## Migration, sacred trees and choke points

### A northern departure, not a featureless arrow

The bird homeland occupies the impact district and surrounding northern country in the draft. Cooling affects nesting sites, food supplies and accessible routes. Families, livestock, freight and institutions do not move like a single airborne flock. [D2, D5]

### Why the passes matter to birds

Flying individuals can cross barriers that stop carts, but high altitude, weather, loads, feeding stops and ground-based supply still create preferred corridors. Western, Central and Eastern Gate are proposed lower passages. They are logistical choke points, not an absolute biological wall.

### Where control becomes visible

Checkpoint stations can cluster at valley mouths, crossings, staging fields and supply depots. Beyond them, routes branch toward timber country, lakeshore towns and the downstream metropolis. The model locates candidate gates; it does not specify a border regime or a completed road network.

### The forest preserves memory

Ancient avian carvings and cliff ruins sit above the western forest corridor. The old-growth refuge provides a setting where material evidence, oral history and present land claims meet. Logging can expose ruins while destroying the living landscape around them. Those are narrative proposals grounded in the deck's sacred-tree material. [D5, D9]

### Historical continuity

The latest brief sets two distinct migrations: an older cat crossing enabled by southern winter, and current birds moving south as northern winter approaches. The 1,100-year crossing date is illustrative. Earlier deck dates, including roughly 700 years of northern occupation, need reconciliation with the chosen epoch; they are not silently treated as confirmed history. [D2, D5, D45]

## Use the globe as a drawing reference

### Choose the angle before drawing

Open the offline atlas and select the hemisphere or regional view. Orthographic cameras show the actual sphere without a map-projection stretch. Land near the limb is foreshortened; choose a closer-centered view when a region is the drawing subject. The annotated versions locate features on the same renders.

### Open the editable models

The globe contains thirteen saved cameras with matching lights, packed textures and named anchors. The dam has two reference views. The waterfall has its own editable local study, with one kilometre per Blender unit and true vertical scale.

### Resolution and exaggeration

An 8192 x 4096 colour map and three 4096 x 2048 physical maps support the globe. Thirteen globe views, two dam views and one waterfall view make sixteen clean and sixteen annotated references. The globe uses 10x relief and widened rivers; local studies use true relief. Small buildings, individual trees and engineering details remain below global resolution.

### What can be edited

Geographic anchors, directed river paths and planet parameters are stored in source JSON. The build pipeline reconstructs earlier terrain stages before applying R6, R7, R8 terrain and R9 ocean geography without its rejected elastic warp, then R10 rigid plate placement, so transforms are not accumulated by repeatedly rotating a finished model. The packed Blender files remain independently editable.

### Light and atmosphere

Clouds are omitted to expose the terrain. Each camera has broad daylight from a useful drawing angle, not one simultaneous astronomical illumination state. The blue limb is a restrained visual atmosphere; it is not an atmospheric scattering simulation.

## Decisions for the creator to make next

### 1 / Heat source and seasonal physics

Keep the adopted 35-degree tilt and northern-autumn/southern-spring direction. Decide the habitable heat source and validate the precise epoch, temperature distribution, ice mass and sea-level history.

### 2 / Homeland and coastline approval

Review the separate southern mainland and eastern archipelago, and decide whether this is officially Old Panterra. Panterra and the eastern continent remain mountain-connected. Final names, factions and political borders remain open.

### 3 / Reservoir and waterfall history

Develop the pre-dam valley, filling timescale, natural western sill, eastern operating rules and the waterfall's resistant bedrock. The local fall is a proposal with a 250 m main drop and 150 m of lower cascades.

### 4 / Energy and material limits

Choose reliable rather than nominal power output, catchment assumptions, industrial demand and the role of scarce iron. Treat naturally enriched uranium and any unusual cooling mechanism as separate fictional premises requiring decisions.

### 5 / Ice budget, crossing and travel

Approve or revise the proposed 65 m lowstand and 1,100-year crossing date. Test net grounded-ice storage, relative sea level and local shelf elevations before declaring a safe historic route. Fix journey distances and seasonal pass accessibility for scripts.

### 6 / Local production detail

Develop Seattle forest districts, agricultural settlements, lake shipping, eastern dam operations and the northern passes at regional scale. Use the broad continental atlas for continuity; commission local maps for individual journeys and scenes.

## Ocean islands and plate boundaries

### Different origins, different shapes

The southern shelf islands are elongated continental fragments and an inherited arc, with drowned saddles between them. Pelagic Crown instead consists of overlapping shield volcanoes above a proposed hotspot. Its two older companions diminish northeastward; the gaps remain deep ocean. Northern fore-islands are rugged remnants of continental crust, with fjords and seasonal summit snow.

### What the tectonic sheet means

Five named plate domains are separated by a conceptual network: collision along the giant mountains; subduction beside the curved arc; spreading ridges through remote oceans; and transform links joining the zones. Teeth mark the proposed overriding side. Motion arrows show direction only. Shelf edges, former migration routes and hotspot trails have separate symbols because they are not automatically plate boundaries. [S14]

### Scale and uncertainty

The large volcanic island is an intentionally oversized fictional analogue, not a copy of Hawaii. The plate domains, polar boundary, motions and volcanic ages are proposals; no Euler-pole reconstruction or geodynamic solution has been calculated. Continental shelves can be thousands of kilometres wide only under special geology; the -65 m migration shelf remains a deliberate story constraint.
