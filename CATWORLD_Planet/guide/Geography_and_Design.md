# CATWORLD / TERRA

Planet and drawing-reference atlas

A coherent globe reconstructed from the latest Catworld sketch, with proposed physical geography. Prepared 31 October 2026. This is a worldbuilding model, not a climate simulation or a final canon document.

## Start here

Open `model/CATWORLD_Terra.blend` for the whole planet. Its surface textures are packed into the file. Open `model/CATWORLD_Dam_Study.blend` for the separate valley-scale dam study. The two files use different scales; the dam is an inset at landmark 05, not a second competing world map.

Use the numbered clean PNGs in `renders/` as drawing references. Matching redline plates in `annotated/` identify the important places. The contact sheet shows the complete set. The globe cameras are orthographic: they give a natural globe view without perspective convergence, although the limb still foreshortens the surface. Center a region in the view you draw from. No single view can show an entire sphere without distortion.

The north and south views look directly down their respective rotation poles. East and west are views centered on +90 and -90 degrees longitude. The Panterra-facing view is centered at 0 degrees longitude, 22 degrees north; the far side is centered at 180 degrees. Longitudes are an invented working coordinate system, not facts implied by the source sketch.

## What is fixed, and what is proposed

The user's current request takes precedence over the deck's development notes. Map 5 is the coastline authority, with its northern horseshoe impact sea, long mountain divide, central reservoir, eastern lobe, southwestern continent and broken island connection. Older maps supply context rather than alternate coastlines. The deck supplies the Flock's northern homeland, Panterra's resource scarcity, great forests, canals, dam and trade economy. The attached conversation supplies fishing settlements, ancient ruins and migration checkpoints.

The user confirmed a roughly Earth-sized or slightly smaller world, an Earth-like axial tilt and a 4,000-year orbital period. This model uses a 6,100 km radius and 23.4-degree tilt. A roughly 24-hour rotation is an explicit working assumption. The present epoch is proposed as northern late autumn and southern spring. At near-circular orbit, each season lasts about 1,000 Earth years. Epoch, ocean currents, winds, exact elevations, river branches, place coordinates and all additional landforms remain proposals.

The hand sketch has no graticule or defined projection. I traced the coastlines, fitted an inverse elliptical/Mollweide-like mapping, then shifted the latitude distribution northward to place the crater around 65 N, the main divide around 30-40 N and the western southern desert around 20-35 S. Following the user's revisions, central Panterra was narrowed by up to 23%, its southern extent shortened slightly, its major capes sharpened and several gulfs deepened. The impact basin was enlarged. The eastern landmass gained a long rift gulf; the southern landmass gained a drowned rift and a distinct convergent range. The far northern remnant gained fjords. Outer map edges were reconciled at the longitude seam. These deliberate exaggerations preserve the recognizable arrangement while giving each landmass a stronger silhouette. This is an interpretable reconstruction, not a mathematically exact inversion of a known projection.

Real-world city names in the drawings remain shorthand. "London," "Amsterdam," "Seattle" and "Milan" are not silently promoted to final Catworld names. Most labels in this atlas describe a function or landform. "Ferric Sea," "Northern Divide," "Western Gate," "Central Gate" and "Eastern Gate" are proposed working labels.

## One world, three controlling systems

### The impact north

The northern horseshoe is the eroded remnant of a very old, enormous impact basin. Its broken northern rim admits seawater; the central island represents part of a rebound uplift. A rusty sediment apron and shallow plumes make the inner sea red-brown. Deep water outside the basin stays blue. Glaciers and tundra are advancing from the north, while less exposed coastal pockets remain habitable.

The impact delivered iron-nickel metal and exposed, melted and fractured the local crust. Subsequent groundwater circulation and erosion redistributed ore into veins, sediment beds and heavy-mineral placers. That combination is more credible than treating every useful element as an intact pile from the asteroid. Iron-rich ejecta are the fictional exception on this otherwise iron-poor world. Rare-earth and uranium concentrations should chiefly be assigned to unusual target rocks and later geochemistry, not automatic products of an ordinary iron meteorite. Naturally enriched uranium in the deck remains a separate speculative premise.

The basin is country to subcontinent scale in this reconstruction. It therefore implies an ancient global catastrophe and a long recovery, well before the present civilizations. The visible ring is weathered and partly drowned, not a fresh impact bowl. A real ancient impact would not retain a pristine high rim indefinitely. The model keeps a legible ring for the drawing reference, with later uplift as a possible explanation for its survival.

### The mountain wall and plateau

The Northern Divide is a separate collision belt south of the crater. It is not the crater's ejecta rim. Its highest modeled ground approaches 8.6 km, with broad foothills, subsidiary ridges, snowfields and lower saddles. The broad mountain belt follows map 5 across the northern continent and into the eastern landmass. Its main uplifted zone is several hundred to roughly a thousand kilometres wide, depending on the transect. Parallel ridges, foreland hills and deep transverse valleys replace the initial thin, noisy summit stripe.

The southern flank descends onto a dissected plateau rather than directly to the ocean. Most uplands are roughly 0.8-1.8 km high; valleys cut down toward the reservoir and coast. Moist air approaching from the south and ocean-facing slopes supports woodland and feeds the rivers. The northern interior loses moisture after air crosses the divide, creating cold steppe and drier basins between the mountains and impact district. The texture transitions are illustrative climate zones, not an atmospheric model.

Western, central and eastern saddles create concentrated crossing routes. They do not form a perfect barrier to flying birds. They matter because lower altitudes, safer winds, shelter, water, food and access for nonflying dependents make some routes much more useful than others. Checkpoints belong at these valleys and supply corridors, not arbitrarily on every summit. Mountain-carved avian ruins occupy cliffs near the western crossing, where migrants can see them from the approach.

### The lake, dam and lowland economy

The central water body retains the latest sketch's broad shape and gains drowned tributary arms. At the chosen planetary scale it covers approximately 1.54 million square kilometres. It is an inland freshwater sea. The proposed normal surface is 690 m above global sea level. A broad structural basin existed here before impoundment; a smaller ancestral lake occupied its deepest valley. Raising the outlet drowned the low basin and connected its tributary valleys.

The dam closes a bedrock throat at the southeastern outlet, not a continent-wide gap. Downstream, the river descends toward the lowland trade outlet identified by the Amsterdam shorthand. Tributary rivers converge on the reservoir; below it, floodplain channels and distributaries spread toward the coast. The lower river carries barges, irrigation supply, timber and commerce. Upper navigation needs locks, lifts or portage around the dam. An uninterrupted flat canal crossing the full head would be impossible.

The dam study proposes a 4.8 km crest, normal reservoir level 690 m, crest 715 m and deepest foundation about 245 m. Structural height is therefore about 470 m, with about 420 m between reservoir and immediate tailwater. A conservative illustrative net generating head is 350 m. Its mass-concrete/stone gravity structure bears the load; bronze and limited iron are reserved for gates, machinery and critical fittings. Aluminum requires an established power and refining economy and should be a later component, not the material that makes the first dam possible. These are visual worldbuilding dimensions, not a validated structural design.

At 90% efficiency, 10,000 cubic metres per second through turbines at 350 m net head yields about 31 GW: P = rho g Q H eta. A proposed 60 GW installed capacity would need roughly 19,400 cubic metres per second at full output. Installed capacity is not continuous output. The dam is far beyond familiar terrestrial examples and requires an exceptional drainage basin, sediment management, flood bypasses and a maintenance state on a national scale.

A sample annual water budget, stated in Earth years, makes the size consequential. A 6 million km2 contributing catchment with 0.40 m/year net runoff supplies 2,400 km3/year. If lake evaporation exceeds direct precipitation by 0.80 m/year, the reservoir loses about 1,235 km3/year, leaving about 1,165 km3/year, or 36,911 m3/s, for releases before other withdrawals. Only part of this should go through turbines continuously. The catchment and fluxes are proposed scenario values, not measured results of the procedural drainage network. A drier climate would require a smaller lake, greater runoff, or less power.

With a hypothetical mean stored depth of 100 m, the lake holds about 154,397 km3. Even a sustained net filling surplus of 25,000 m3/s takes roughly 196 years. That fits a civilization building through a millennium-long season, but rules out a casual recent project. Shoreline displacement, drowned towns, old terraces, sediment deltas, fisheries and navigation become major story facts. A dam cannot create freshwater indefinitely without positive inflow and an outlet that exports salts.

## Why the sharper continent shapes belong here

The working tectonic sequence has three stages. First, an old northern craton receives the ancient impact. Second, convergence between that craton and the Panterran plate closes an older ocean and builds a long collision suture. Eastern terranes join through oblique convergence and strike-slip deformation, producing the broad mountain knot where the belt turns southeast. Third, younger extension exploits inherited weaknesses and opens narrow rift gulfs on the eastern landmass and southern shelf.

This explains contrasting coast types. Broad passive margins retain lowlands and river mouths. Fault-guided gulfs create deep angular bites. Resistant basement blocks form sharp capes. Flooded valleys make branching coves. Long peninsulas remain between adjacent rift arms. High-latitude ice cuts fjords. The impact horseshoe has a different origin and should not be mirrored by arbitrary circular features elsewhere.

The southern desert continent has its own older shield, eastern convergent mountain spine and lower oblique range. It is not just spare land on the back of the globe. Dry basins, gravel plains, dune corridors and occasional pale salt pans occupy the shield; wetter southern slopes and eastern drainage face the ocean. Its drowned shelf gulf breaks the northeast coast into capes and islands. The eastern continent has a humid outer coast, the eastern collision knot, a deep rift gulf and long coastal peninsulas. Both have dedicated regional renders in addition to the hemisphere views.

The tectonic framework plate summarizes these proposals. It does not claim to reconstruct plate velocities, a closed global plate circuit or a simulated geological history. Broad landform causes are coherent; exact faults, timings and political boundaries remain open.

## Regional geography and visual cues

### Panterra's western forest corridor

The west-facing foothills around landmark 07 receive moist maritime air and runoff from the divide. Deep valleys and sheltered slopes hold the oldest forest refuges; younger woodland and cleared strips occupy accessible river valleys. Giant trees are a biological premise from the deck. The globe shows their forest cover, not individual continent-sized trunks. Draw very large trees only at local scale, concentrated in protected moist valleys. Logging roads, cut blocks and downstream timber depots make the economy visible without covering the globe in oversized city icons.

Ancient cliff ruins sit above navigable water and near old passage routes. This explains why the birds recognize the place and why later Panterran commerce occupies the same corridor. A timber port belongs at the river mouth, while sawmills occupy tributary falls or powered canal branches. "Seattle" is the functional reference for this region, not its final name.

### Plateau, reservoir shores and Pieter's village

The plateau is a patchwork of tributary valleys, grassland, weathered uplands and cultivated lower slopes. The reservoir's tributary mouths have deltas and shallow fishing grounds; sheltered coves support small municipalities. Exposed capes and steep drowned valleys remain sparsely settled. Draw shoreline terraces above the water to record past filling stages, with old roads disappearing beneath the lake.

Pieter's settlement is provisionally placed near the dam's outlet district, close enough for the story's dam conflict but away from the crest itself. The local model shows an illustrative fishing settlement on an upstream terrace. That cove is a detailed design proposal, not resolvable geometry on the 4K globe. Its precise street plan and position can move without changing the world coastline.

### The lowland trade outlet and southern bay

Landmark 06 is the downstream commercial outlet. Its strength is transport and exchange rather than abundant local ore: branching channels, canals, warehouses, workshops, bridge crossings and embanked farmland are the useful drawing cues. Floodplains need room around the main river. Dense settlement belongs on levees, terraces and engineered ground rather than in every marsh.

The southern bay around landmark 16 is the London shorthand: sheltered water, productive lowlands and access to several routes. At the chosen coordinates its climate is warm; European cultural references do not require European latitude. Vineyards would favor elevated, seasonally drier slopes, while lowland fields support crops suited to the local long season. The merchant island at landmark 17 is the Milan shorthand, controlling a strait and transshipment route. Its shipping role follows from geography, not from naming it after a real city.

### Southern desert and the broken land bridge

The large western interior of the southwestern continent is dry. An added eastern mountain spine intercepts moist easterly trade flow, while subtropical subsidence and a proposed cool western boundary current help keep the western coast arid. The source sketch's oblique southern range remains. The new spine joins it to form a coherent barrier rather than moving the desert to the wrong side.

Dry terrain grades from rocky piedmont and alluvial fans into gravel plains, dune basins and occasional salt pans. The desert should not be drawn as uniform dunes. Seasonal channels terminate inland or cross to the coast only during unusual floods. Farther south, the wind regime changes: western coastal ground becomes cooler and wetter. A single rain-shadow rule cannot be applied unchanged from the tropics to the pole.

The northeast islands preserve the old land-bridge idea. Treat them as exposed summits and shelf fragments left by earlier lower sea level and tectonic fragmentation. Narrow straits and stepping-stone harbors support migration memory and later trade. A continuous bridge in the last few centuries would require an additional specified tectonic or sea-level event; it is not assumed merely because the islands line up.

### Eastern lands and polar oceans

The eastern lobe remains tied to the long mountain system. Its humid ocean-facing lowlands carry forest, river plains and estuaries; more sheltered interior ground is drier. The sketches differ over exactly which reptile groups occupy which region. This atlas preserves the landforms without settling factional borders by inference.

The north has fragmented high-latitude land and ocean ice. The south is mainly ocean in this reconstruction, with maritime seasonal ice rather than a newly invented Antarctic continent. The older "Antarctica" shorthand is interpreted through the latest sketch's northern-edge land remnant; it is not added as a contradictory large southern landmass. Polar shorelines and sea-ice extents are the least constrained part of the source and should stay revisable.

## A 4,000-year year changes the story

With an Earth-like tilt, the astronomical seasonal cycle reverses between hemispheres over thousands of years. A northern winter lasts many human lifetimes; above the polar circle, even the interval without a daily sunrise can last centuries. Most of Panterra still has daily sunrise and sunset under the assumed 24-hour rotation. Far-northern winter darkness and prolonged cooling make the migration compelling without an instantaneous global freeze.

Oceans store heat, but they do not erase a millennium of changed sunlight. Ice margins, tree lines, monsoon belts and viable cropland can move over generations. Reservoir design must anticipate centuries of altered inflow rather than an ordinary annual cycle. The render shows one proposed epoch; its ice and vegetation are art-directed indicators, not an equilibrium solution. "Year" in character dialogue should be defined: people will need a shorter civil calendar for ages, harvests, accounts and historical dates.

There is an unresolved astronomical constraint. For a one-solar-mass primary, Kepler's third law puts a 4,000-Earth-year orbit at about 252 AU. An ordinary Sun at that distance cannot provide Earth-like warmth. This atlas preserves the user's orbital period and assumes a fictional illumination/heat arrangement. A luminous primary, a different multiple-body configuration or another explicit setting mechanism needs separate design; none is silently declared physically solved here. Recasting the 4,000 years as a climate cycle instead would be an alternate canon choice, not what this model assumes.

The deck's industrial "inverse greenhouse" cooling is also separate from this seasonal clock. Aerosols and changes in reflectivity can cool regions; ordinary greenhouse-gas accumulation alone does not explain inverse greenhouse cooling. A later climate treatment should identify the actual forcing, magnitude and duration. The map does not require the industrial explanation to be settled before the northern autumn migration can exist.

## How to draw from these references

1. Choose one main globe view. For Panterra, use the front view for context and the regional close view for coast and terrain placement. Use the other hemispheres to resolve features approaching the limb.
2. Lightly trace the coastline, reservoir shore and principal watershed first. Keep the circular crater and the separate southern mountain belt distinct.
3. Add tributaries from high ground toward larger rivers and water bodies. Use the generated network as a visual guide, then simplify at your drawing scale. It is a priority-flood terrain routing proposal, not a survey; small channels and individual drainage divides can be revised.
4. Draw mountain chains as families of ridges and valleys. The globe uses fivefold radial relief exaggeration to make terrain visible. Do not use the rendered apparent mountain height to calculate actual world dimensions.
5. Place forests, dry basins and cultivated strips in response to water and elevation. Put settlements at crossings, harbors, tributary mouths and stable shore terraces.
6. Use the dam detail only for local engineering and visual storytelling. The true globe-scale dam is smaller than a cleanly readable feature in these images. Red callouts identify its position without pretending it is hundreds of kilometres wide.

## Model and reproducibility notes

The globe has approximately 525,000 quad faces, a 4,096 x 2,048 albedo map, a 16-bit height texture, a roughness map and named geographic landmark empties. Terrain fields store physical heights in metres. Radius is 6,100 km; the Blender display sphere uses radius 10 units with fivefold vertical exaggeration. Surface details below a roughly 9 km equatorial texture pixel are illustrative shading rather than resolved geography. The dam scene uses 1 unit = 1 km and separate local coordinates, with no vertical exaggeration.

All main renders use the same globe mesh and packed textures. No view independently invents new coastlines. Cameras and their landmark projections are saved with the source. The light follows the view for legibility, so these are separately illuminated atlas references rather than simultaneous photographs of a fixed Sun. Clean globes have transparent backgrounds; annotated plates use a dark field for contrast. Clouds are omitted so important details remain visible.

The scripts create coast geometry, background fractal terrain, major relief, climate color zones, a routed tributary network, materials, cameras and the local dam study. Real Himalayan elevation data supplies an eroded ridge-and-valley vocabulary: it is warped along the invented northern suture and southern convergent margin, rescaled in height, and blended into the fictional terrain. It is not represented as a measured elevation map of Catworld. The process is deterministic with the recorded seeds. It does not solve tectonics, coupled ocean-atmosphere climate, long-term glacial erosion, reservoir structural safety or sediment transport. It is a detailed, editable geographic concept suitable for drawing reference and further art direction.
