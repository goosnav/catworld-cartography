# CATWORLD / TERRA — REVISION 2

Planet and drawing-reference atlas. Prepared 31 October 2026. This revision follows the user's corrections to the three-continent mountain connection, far-side climate, polar geography and Seattle waterway. Names and physical details that were not specified remain proposals.

## The revised geographic contract

There are THREE major connected continental cores: Panterra, the eastern/far-side continental interior, and the southern maritime core. The huge orogenic system links all three through broad mountain necks. “Connected” does not mean three isolated islands with unrelated ranges. The land connection and mountain relief continue across the longitude seam on the opposite hemisphere.

The marked far-side screenshot controls the climate correction. Its large green region on the left becomes the broad dry interior. Its tan region on the right becomes the wetter maritime core. A long archipelago continues from the right-hand core toward Panterra. It represents fragments of a former continental shelf connection, rather than randomly scattered oceanic dots.

Panterra's recognizable outline and position are retained. The northern impact basin now has a wider geological apron merging into the continent. The main mountain system has a much broader elevated core, parallel ranges and snowy high plateaus. The polar regions have separate land, water and ice structures. The original sketch still governs the world's overall arrangement; the new screenshot and explicit corrections take precedence over earlier interpretations.

The confirmed waterway order is: SEATTLE → PRIMARY RIVER → SUPER-LAKE → DAM → DOWNSTREAM BIG CITY. London and Rome are positioned near the lake. The big city below the dam remains the Amsterdam shorthand from the reference material. These real-world city names are working labels, not an assertion that Terra duplicates Earth.

## Three cores, one enormous mountain system

Panterra occupies the main drawing hemisphere, with its forested western uplands, huge reservoir basin, cultivated southern lowlands and northern wall. Across the eastern mountain neck lies the second core: a large old continental interior with an eastern mountain flank, dry basins and deeply indented coasts. Beyond the far-side collision isthmus lies the third core, a wetter southern landmass with river lowlands, coastal forests, broken capes and a major highland spine.

The proposed history has two successive ocean closures. First, Panterra and the eastern terranes converge, thickening the crust along the Northern Divide. Later, a southern continental block docks against the far end of this system. The younger collision joins the existing belt at an oblique angle and turns it onto the third core. Wide mountain necks are the exposed suture zones between the old continental interiors. Their embayments are surviving or subsequently reopened marginal basins.

The model therefore contains an enormous connected belt, not a thin string of isolated peaks. The high core is several hundred kilometres wide, with broader foothills and plateau transitions. Peaks reach a proposed 10.8 km; extensive terrain lies several kilometres above sea level. Those extreme dimensions are a fantasy-world design choice. They are not derived from a lithospheric strength or erosion simulation.

Real Himalayan elevation data supplies ridge-and-valley structure, warped to this invented belt and combined with new elevation envelopes. The real data is an analogue for erosion, not evidence that the fictional plate history occurred. The numerical elevation field is in metres. Radial relief is exaggerated TEN times on the globe so the ranges read at planetary scale. This display exaggeration must not be used to infer literal cliff heights.

The passages through the Northern Divide are lower saddles within the range. They remain natural places for migration checkpoints, supply stations and fortified crossings. Airborne birds can cross terrain that constrains carts and heavy freight; the passes are logistical choke points rather than an absolute biological barrier.

## Why the far side has these climates

The dry left-hand interior lies behind the broad mountain wall and its eastern subsidiary range. Moist air from the exposed northern/eastern seas rises on the ocean-facing slopes, loses moisture, and descends toward the inland basins. Continental distance and a proposed cool coastal current reinforce the dry western margins. This supports gravel deserts, dune basins, seasonal wadis and pale salt flats instead of a uniformly green expanse.

The right-hand southern core is exposed to warm maritime easterlies. Its outer lowlands and island-facing coast intercept moisture before it reaches the high spine. Forest and river plains grade upward into cooler upland woodland and alpine terrain. Smaller sheltered dry pockets are possible, but the broad region marked by the user is now the wet side. The transition is gradual and follows the terrain and maritime exposure; it does not stop at the arbitrary map seam.

This is an explicit circulation proposal for the illustrated epoch. A rain shadow depends on the direction of the moisture-bearing winds, not merely which side of the page a mountain occupies. The diagram marks the proposed incoming moisture, the barriers and the lee basin. The climate field is an art-directed heuristic; no general circulation model or ocean simulation has been run.

Near Panterra, the wet western uplands support the old-growth timber country. Valleys collect runoff from the high range and feed the Seattle river. The plateau and lower basin contain a mix of forest, open country and agriculture. Forest cover is deliberately broken by height, substrate and moisture rather than drawn as one solid green patch.

## The long island bridge

The archipelago arcs from the southern maritime core through elongated shelf islands toward Panterra's western/southwestern approaches. Larger islands preserve fragments of older continental crust; smaller islands and reefs mark the drowned connecting saddles. Straits, embayments and submerged ridges give the chain a consistent direction.

The seabed along the proposed connecting shelf is approximately 65–130 m below the present sea surface. A lower sea stand could expose substantial stretches of it. This makes an old land-bridge migration route plausible as a proposal; it does not establish that every channel was simultaneously dry, nor fix the date of a crossing. The reference sketch's historical migration notes need a separate chronology before an exact event can be declared canon.

Fishing towns belong on sheltered sounds and productive shelf edges. Merchant stations belong at the few narrow passages that deep-draft vessels can use. Ruins can occupy stranded headlands or former road approaches above drowned saddles. These sites follow resources and movement routes instead of being evenly scattered decorations.

## Seattle, the super-lake and the downstream metropolis

Seattle is an upstream timber and river city within the western uplands. Mountain tributaries converge above it. The primary river leaves the Seattle district, crosses the foreland valley and enters the western side of the super-lake. Its grade falls continuously toward the lake's 690 m water level. The lake is not the source of the Seattle river.

The main channel has been made much more visible than its secondary tributaries. Its width is exaggerated for drawing-reference readability: a several-pixel channel on a 4K world texture is not a measured river tens of kilometres wide. The numerical model includes a separately graded water surface over a shallow bed. The source hydrology verification records its descending profile. This checks the intended main direction; it does not certify every procedural tributary or a full flood model.

London lies beside the lake's southwestern agricultural hinterland; Rome is proposed near the eastern shore. They can draw on reservoir fisheries, water transport and irrigated land. The lake's controlled outlet passes the great dam, then descends to the alluvial metropolis identified as Amsterdam in the reference deck. That downstream city benefits from trade, canals, processing and power rather than requiring its own rich mineral hinterland.

The reservoir is a continental super-lake, approximately 1.54 million square kilometres in this reconstruction. That size is retained from the earlier basin interpretation. A single dam can control such a lake only if a naturally enclosed basin already exists and one principal bedrock outlet controls its level. The dam does not manufacture a continental depression. The extensive drowned tributary valleys and shoreline benches represent the flooded landscape.

The local dam model is unchanged in overall arrangement: a 4.8 km crest, crest elevation about 715 m, reservoir at 690 m, and a much lower downstream gorge. Gates, spillways, turbine halls, access roads, checkpoints and a nearby settlement make the scale readable. It is a conceptual engineering reference, not a construction design. A net working head of 350 m is an illustrative power calculation, not a solved hydraulic profile.

For scale only, P = rho × g × Q × H × efficiency gives about 31 GW for 10,000 cubic metres per second, 350 m head and 90% efficiency. A nominal 60 GW installation would require roughly 19,400 cubic metres per second at the same head. Storage area alone does not guarantee those flows. A six-million-square-kilometre catchment receiving 0.4 m of annual runoff yields 2,400 cubic kilometres per year; 0.8 m of net evaporation from the lake removes about 1,235 cubic kilometres. Those assumed values leave about 1,165 cubic kilometres for annual release. None of those runoff or evaporation assumptions has been established by a climate simulation.

At an assumed 100 m mean depth the lake would store about 154,000 cubic kilometres. Even at 25,000 cubic metres per second of net inflow, filling from empty would take roughly two centuries. A more credible history begins with existing water and a partially flooded basin, with the dam expanding and stabilizing it over a long construction and filling period.

## The impact district belongs to the continent

The Ferric Sea remains the large, old northern impact basin. Its rusty shore and apron merge into surrounding continental rock through eroded foothills, uplands and buried ejecta. The ring is not a separate circular island attached by a narrow stalk. The southern apron broadens into the craton, while the northern breach and central uplift remain readable.

Red-brown sediment suggests oxidized iron minerals such as hematite. It is a useful visual cue for the local iron-rich province, but red colour alone is not proof of meteoritic origin. The sea is coloured by suspended sediment and staining, not by a liquid layer of metallic iron. Ore-bearing impact deposits and later hydrothermal redistribution provide a proposed basis for the resource district. Rare-earth resources should not be treated as an automatic consequence of every iron-rich meteorite.

The impact is older than the present collision belt. Its crater ring and the Northern Divide have different causes. Later tectonics, weathering and erosion partly bury and modify the old impact structure. A basin this large implies an exceptionally destructive ancient event; the world needs enough subsequent time for climate and ecosystems to recover.

## The two poles have different identities

The north is an oceanic polar region with bedrock islands, glaciated uplands, channels, broken pack ice and a seasonally open connection toward the impact district. Ice fractures form irregular floes and leads. Island ice has relief and exposed coastal rock, while floating sea ice follows the ocean surface. The cap is no longer a plain white latitude circle.

The south contains a separate polar landmass, distinct from the three inhabited connected cores. Its outline is asymmetric, with a long peninsula, deep embayments, coastal mountains and an interior ice dome. Grounded ice flows toward the coast, and patchier marine ice occupies adjacent shelves. The pole itself is continuous terrain; texture detail is computed in local polar coordinates to avoid radial pinching and spoke-like artefacts.

This north-ocean/south-land contrast is inspired by Earth's different polar settings. It is a proposed geography for Terra, not an assertion that its ice must match Earth's present extent. The ice margins and colour variations are designed visual reference features rather than outputs of an ice-sheet model.

## Long seasons remain a major worldbuilding constraint

The user specified a roughly Earth-sized planet, Earth-like axial tilt, and an orbital period of 4,000 Earth years. This model uses a 6,100 km radius, 23.4-degree tilt and an assumed 24-hour rotation. On a near-circular orbit the four seasonal quarters each last about 1,000 Earth years. The illustrated epoch remains northern late autumn / southern spring as a working choice.

These seasons are long enough for forests, rivers, soils, ice margins and settlements to adjust over generations. They should not be written as Earth seasons simply played in slow motion. A civilization may know only a fraction of a seasonal cycle within its recorded history. The model depicts one epoch, not the planet's entire four-millennia climate cycle.

A 4,000-year orbit around a one-solar-mass star has a semimajor axis of roughly 252 AU. A Sun-like star would not provide Earth-like warmth at that distance. The setting therefore still needs an explicit stellar, orbital or additional-heating premise. This atlas preserves the user's orbital period and labels the unresolved thermal premise instead of silently substituting an Earth year.

## How this revision was checked

Draft globe passes were inspected from Panterra, the far side, both poles, the Seattle valley and the island bridge. The review specifically looked for a continuous far-side range, reversed biome placement, coherent coast transitions at the map seam, irregular ice rather than repeated patterns, and a visible Seattle-to-reservoir river. The final camera set covers all hemispheres and the two additional regional studies.

The source data records physical elevations separately from the 10× display relief. Named landmarks are editable objects in Blender and are listed with coordinates in the catalogue. The annotated plates use the same saved camera projections as their clean renders. The globe's four surface textures are packed into the model.

Limits: the terrain is a detailed worldbuilding reference at a planetary scale. Individual houses, trees, narrow rivers and dam structures cannot be physically resolved on a 4K world texture. The dam is supplied as a separate local model. Plate motions, climate, ice flow and hydraulic engineering are reasoned proposals rather than numerical simulations. A hand-drawn sketch without a graticule cannot be inverted into one uniquely correct globe.
